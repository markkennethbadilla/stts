# Speech engine

## What it does

Turns what Mark says into text on this computer, with no network. The voice window sends the daemon its microphone audio; the daemon finds where speech starts and stops and writes down each stretch of speech. The page receives the text exactly as it used to receive it from Chrome, so the turn rules (specs 002 and 005), the End button and every reply format are unchanged.

## Why it exists

Chrome's built-in speech recognition lost his words (Mark 2026-10-07): sessions that started late cut off the start of his sentences, restarts threw away speech in flight, and a two-minute turn came back as one word. Mark: "replace the stt with something else that works and doesn't drop my speech". Chosen after a search (2026-10-07): sherpa-onnx (k2-fsa, Apache-2.0, about 15,000 stars, active), the most-used local engine that does both voice detection and recognition from Node with no Python. The model is NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 (int8). On two minutes each of two Filipino-accented call-centre recordings (AIxBlock set, human transcripts) it scored 12.8% and 30.5% word error, most of the second from number formatting ("2019" for "twenty nineteen"); a two-minute recording decoded in about 2 seconds.

The model is now Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 (int8), the one blessed transcription model (mkb-winops spec 061): the plugin reads the single shared copy at `A:\models\parakeet\parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-int8` and keeps no model of its own (the v2 copy was 631 MB per Windows account). sherpa-onnx cannot read those files (it wants the decoder and joiner as two files), so sherpa-onnx is kept for voice detection only and the words come from onnx-asr (istupakov, MIT, the package that publishes those files), run in a Python worker. On 25 spoken test sentences cut the way the engine cuts them (26 segments, 98 live partial clips) v3 left no segment empty and scored 2 word errors in 259 against 4 for v2 (9 before the trailing silence described below was added).

## How it works

- **Page.** One echo-cancelled, noise-suppressed microphone stream is opened once (spec 006) and fed through an AudioWorklet (`web/pcm-worklet.js`, bundled into the page so capture never waits on the daemon) at 16 kHz, in 100 ms blocks, over a WebSocket to the daemon's `/asr`. Audio captured while that socket is down is kept and sent when it reconnects. `web/daemonRecognition.ts` has the shape of Chrome's `SpeechRecognition`, so `startMic` and the page machine are unchanged; its audio pipe lives for the whole page, so a recogniser restart never stops the audio, and text that arrives while no recogniser is started is delivered to the next one. Nothing he says is dropped.
- **Daemon.** `src/asr.ts`: the model loads once when the daemon starts (loaded on the first connection, it blocked the daemon for 8 to 14 s and the start of his speech was lost, live test 2026-10-07). It loads in a worker process, `asr_worker.py` beside `daemon.js`, run by the Python of Piper's venv with the onnx-asr package: about 5 s, and the daemon is not blocked meanwhile. The daemon writes each clip to the worker's stdin (a 4-byte length, then 16 kHz float32 samples) and reads one JSON line back; replies come in the order asked. The worker ends when the daemon does. A worker that exits is logged (`asr worker FAIL` with its last error line), clips waiting on it come back with no words, and it is started again on the next clip, at most once every 30 s; a page that connects while it is down gets `{"type":"error"}` naming the failure and the install script (onnx-asr missing from the venv is the usual cause). A clear clip that decodes to nothing is decoded once more without its first 0.1 s (a known Parakeet TDT fault, NeMo issue 15757; it rescued 2 of 2 empty live clips in the test set). A segment that still decodes to nothing after its live words were shown is sent as those live words (log `decode empty, live words kept`): in the first live session on v3 a 2.9 s segment came back empty after "It's probably" had been shown. Each segment is sent with 0.3 s of silence after it: without it v3 added words nobody said to one segment ("it's not the same") and dropped a word from another. Each `/asr` connection gets its own Silero voice detector (threshold 0.5, 0.25 s minimum speech, 0.5 s silence ends a segment, 20 s maximum). Each segment is decoded with the 0.3 s of audio before it (the detector marks a start just after the first word begins; without it "colour tiles" came back as "tiles") by the one shared Parakeet worker, one after another, so text arrives in the order he spoke. The socket answers `{"type":"speechstart"}` when speech begins and `{"type":"final","text":...}` per segment. A missing engine answers `{"type":"error"}` once, naming the install script, and the daemon logs `asr <message>`.
- **Live words.** While the voice detector says he is speaking, the engine decodes the utterance so far about every 0.4 s (from 0.6 s before the detected start; one decode at a time, a busy engine skips a round) and sends `{"type":"interim","text":...}` when the text changed. The page shows it at once as shimmering words, so he sees each word as he says it, as in Gemini's voice input. Interim words restart the auto-send hold, confirm a barge and tell the daemon he is talking (`talking`, spec 002). A recogniser restart never keeps interim words as heard: the audio pipe outlives it and the final still comes.
- **Sounds with no words.** A segment that decodes to nothing, or a sound too short to become a segment, answers `{"type":"nowords"}`. The page resumes speech it had paused for that sound and clears the turn's start time. Before this the engine said nothing, the agent's voice stayed paused for the full 20 s hold ("sometimes you just stop talking": a 17 s reply took 40 s, log 2026-11-13 00:11 UTC) and the next turn's start time was that sound's ("heard 08:07:45 to 08:09:03" for four words).
- **Log.** Every segment logs `asr segment N.Ns decoded in MSms, W words` and every wordless sound `asr sound with no speech segment`, never the words, so a stretch where he spoke and nothing was heard shows as no segment lines at all (mic or level) or as segments with 0 words (recogniser).
- **Turns.** Final text arrives per segment, at a pause or after 20 s of speech. A segment ends at half a second of silence, but the turn does not: the page's autosend hold (spec 005) still decides when his turn is over, so a pause only splits his words into segments that join into one turn.
- **Echo.** Echo cancellation on the stream removes the agent's voice; the page's text echo check (spec 005) still drops anything that matches what the agent just said, checked against each recent clip and against the recent clips joined in order: on a phone speaker the echo runs on with no pause, so one heard segment spans several clips and matched none alone (2026-10-13). With barge-in off (spec 006) the mic track is muted while the agent speaks, so no echo reaches the engine at all.
- **Listens never clear words.** Opening a listen (an `stt`, or the end of a `tts` with `listen`) never clears words already heard: the mic stays on between listens, and clearing there sent 9 of 58 words (2026-10-07). Only a sent turn clears them. A listen that opens while the mic already runs turns the light green (`REQUEST` in the turn region); every way the mic stops clears its running flag, so the light never goes green with no recogniser behind it (it did after a speak-only `tts`, 2026-10-07).
- **Tests.** The e2e suite injects a fake recogniser as `globalThis.__sttsRecognition`; the page uses it in place of the daemon engine.

## What it reads and writes

Reads the microphone (through Chrome); the shared model folder `parakeet\parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-int8` under the models folder (`STTS_MODELS_DIR`, else `modelsDir` in `config.json`; `A:\models` on Mark's machines), never written; and, in the data folder: `asr/node_modules/sherpa-onnx-node` (pinned 1.13.8, voice detection only), `models/silero_vad.onnx` and the Python of `piper/venv` with onnx-asr (pinned in mkb-agentops `versions.json`). The plugin ships without `node_modules` and a native addon cannot be bundled, so these are installed like Piper, by mkb-agentops `scripts/setup-stts.ps1`; the model folder is filled by mkb-winops `scripts/blessed-models.ps1`. Writes nothing; audio is never stored.

## How to run, check and hand over

`npm test` runs `test/unit/asr.test.ts`: a missing engine reports how to install it, and, where the engine is installed, the recording `test/fixtures/phoebe.wav` streamed in odd-sized chunks comes back word for word, with live words before the first segment and no word that was not said. To check by hand, run the built daemon on a spare port (`STTS_PORT=15990 STTS_LIVE_UPDATE=0 node dist/daemon.js`) and stream a 16 kHz recording into `ws://127.0.0.1:15990/asr` as float32 blocks; finals print within about a second of each pause. The model changes only when the blessed transcription row in mkb-winops `data/models/blessed.json` changes: then change `MODEL` and `ONNX_ASR_NAME` in `src/asr.ts` to that row's `id` and `onnxAsr`, and rerun the test.
