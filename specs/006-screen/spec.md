# Screen

## What it does

The voice window Mark sees (`web/App.tsx`): a large coloured orb that shows whose turn it is, his words as he speaks, the agent's words as they are spoken, and a small row of controls. It listens with the browser's on-device speech recognition and plays the agent's voice.

## Why it exists

He talks hands-free and glances at the window. Colour and sound tell him the state at a glance; the only text is the transcript.

## How it works

- **Orb** (elevenlabs/ui) tinted by the turn: grey not listening, green speak now, amber heard, amber-brown background result, blue agent speaking. A ripple (magicui) shows while listening and a live waveform while speaking. A shrinking ring counts down the auto-send. Interim words shimmer (motion-primitives). A turn-number chip and one status icon (lucide). The header, the orb, the words, the wave strip and the keyboard box each have their own space, so nothing overlaps at any window size: the orb sits in its own row (its own column at 1200 px and wider) and shrinks to fit it, the words take at most half the height and scroll past it, the wave strip keeps its 96 px, and the header buttons wrap to a second line in a thin strip. There is no minimum width: the screenshot check covers 200, 240, 390, 462x689 (his window), 800x320 and 1600x600 and fails on any overlap. The words use fluid type, `clamp()` on the window width.
- **Buttons:** every button shows its label as a tooltip. Skip (or Esc) stops the speech at once and carries on as if it had finished: a reply with a listen opens the listen, a plain reply returns. Stop still ends the reply and the listen. Repeat plays the last reply again from the window alone (Mark 2026-10-10: to save agent tokens): the same local Piper voice re-synthesises it, the daemon is never told, so no turn reaches the agent and an open listen stays open (its no-speech timer restarts when the repeat ends). It is greyed out while speech plays and before any reply. Stop or Skip during a repeat only silences it; words spoken over it go out as a plain turn with no barge line; a new reply from the agent cuts it short. A read-aloud repeats only its last part.
- **Turn cues** (settings, `__stts__cues`): spoken words by default, "Speak." when a listen opens after the agent's turn and "Stop." when his turn is sent, in the window's Piper voice; or the chimes; or off. Never on an automatic mic restart, and never holding the mic: if Piper has not made the word within 1.5 s the chime plays instead. A cue word goes into the spoken log and restarts the 3 s echo tail, so the mic never takes it for his words. Cue volume is the old earcon volume.
- **Themes:** System (follows the OS light or dark setting, the default), Light or Dark, chosen in settings and saved as `__stts__theme`; applied before first paint. Colours come from the shadcn tokens (`background`, `foreground`, `muted-foreground`, `card`) in `web/index.css`, the font is Public Sans (rule 62). `<meta name="darkreader-lock">` keeps the Dark Reader extension off the window.
- **Controls:** Mute/Unmute, Stop (stops speech and reports `stopped{part}`), Settings, End conversation (sends `ended`; the daemon answers `close` and the window shuts at once, spec 003). Stop and End only act on a real click (`isTrusted`). No spoken word operates the window.
- **Keys:** Ctrl+M or Ctrl+R mute and unmute; Alt+Up and Alt+Down browse history (50 each of his prompts and the agent's replies).
- **Message box:** always shown (Mark 2026-10-10; the old Keyboard / AudioLines toggle is gone). A click anywhere in the window that is not on a control (a button, a field, a menu) puts the cursor in it, so he never has to aim for the box; a drag that selected words to copy keeps its selection. A shadcn Textarea sits at the bottom (placeholder "Message (Enter sends)"; under 360 px wide the Send button drops under it at full width): Enter or the Send button sends, Shift+Enter is a new line, Alt+Up and Alt+Down fill it from his prompt history. Typing while the agent speaks cuts the speech off (barge-in, spec 005).
- **Settings popover:** voice, rate, auto-send, hold, mic picker, earcons and their volume, raise-on-request (off by default). Saved in `localStorage` under the old `__stts__*` keys so earlier settings carry over.
- **Listening:** The daemon's speech engine (spec 014), fed from one echo-cancelled, noise-suppressed mic stream that is opened once and reused by every start (reopened only when its track dies or the chosen mic changes). Opening a new stream before every start lost his first words, and Chrome's own capture (start() with no track) has no echo cancellation, so the agent's reply came back as his turn (Mark 2026-10-07). A turn's start time is its first speechstart or first words, reset after every turn. The listen-open chime plays first, so it is never recorded.
- **Speaking:** text is cut into sentences (spec 004) and packed into clips of at most 120 characters, and each clip is fetched from `/voice/clip` (Piper, default voice `en_GB-jenny_dioco-medium`); the next 3 clips are fetched while one plays, so the first word waits for one short clip only and clips play with no gap. On load the page calls `/voice/warm` so Piper loads its model before the first reply. The settings popover lists the Piper voices from `/voice/list` first, then the Windows voices. A saved voice that is not an installed Piper voice (an old Windows pick) still speaks with the Piper default. Only when the clip request itself fails does the browser's own voice speak it, and the page logs `voice fallback REASON` first, so a fallback is never silent. While the browser voice speaks, spoken barge-in is off: it plays outside the page's audio, so echo cancellation cannot remove it and its own words cut it off.
- **Earcons:** Kenney Interface Sounds (CC0) in `public/earcon/`: `listen-open`, `turn-captured`, `background-result`, one per state change.
- The 1600x600 strip and a 390 wide phone layout come from Tailwind container queries.

The status icon turns red when the mic has failed (spec 005).

## What it reads and writes

Reads the mic and the daemon's WebSocket messages. Writes `localStorage` settings and history, and sends page messages (spec 001) back to the daemon.

## How to run, check and hand over

`npm run dev` serves the page with Vite. `test/screenshots/shoot.ts` produces the screenshots in `test/screenshots/` at 1600x600 and 390 wide; check them for layout and for no console errors. `test/unit/page-log.test.ts` checks the page's log lines.

The window icon is the lucide `audio-waveform` glyph (ISC licence) in blue `#3b82f6` on a transparent background, with no tile (Mark 2026-10-06: the old orb on a dark square looked dated). Source is `public/favicon.svg`; `favicon.ico` (16, 24, 32, 48, 64 px), `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png` and `apple-touch-icon.png` were generated from it with the `favicons` npm package 7.3.1 (not a dependency), and `icon-256.png` with the `sharp` it brings. `index.html` and `manifest.webmanifest` link them with a `?v=N` that is bumped with every icon change (Chrome keeps a window's icon by URL, so the taskbar kept the old one after a reload), and the Chrome `--app` window takes its taskbar icon from them; it shows on the next window open. To change the icon, edit the SVG and rerun both.
