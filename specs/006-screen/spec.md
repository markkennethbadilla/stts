# Screen

## What it does

The voice window Mark sees (`web/App.tsx`): a small coloured orb that shows whose turn it is, his words as he speaks, the agent's words as they are spoken (lit line by line, like Spotify's lyrics view), and a small row of controls. It listens with the browser's on-device speech recognition and plays the agent's voice.

## Why it exists

He talks hands-free and glances at the window. Colour and sound tell him the state at a glance; the only text is the transcript.

## How it works

- **Orb** (elevenlabs/ui) tinted by the turn: grey not listening, green speak now, purple agent working, amber heard, amber-brown background result, blue agent speaking. A ripple (magicui) shows while listening and a live waveform while speaking. A shrinking ring counts down the auto-send. Interim words shimmer (motion-primitives). A turn-number chip and one status icon (lucide). The header, the orb, the words, the wave strip and the keyboard box each have their own space, so nothing overlaps at any window size: the orb sits in its own row (its own column at 1200 px and wider) and shrinks to fit it, the orb is small (Mark 2026-10-10): its row gets a third of the space and the orb is never wider than 12rem (192 px; it filled its row, about 340 px in the 1600x600 strip and 215 px in his 462x689 window), the words get the other two thirds and scroll inside it, the wave strip keeps its 96 px, and the header buttons wrap to a second line in a thin strip. There is no minimum width: the screenshot check covers 200, 240, 390, 462x689 (his window), 800x320 and 1600x600 and fails on any overlap. The words use fluid type, `clamp()` on the window width.
- **Lyrics view** (Mark 2026-10-10, like Spotify's lyrics): the words are split into the same lines the voice speaks, the short first clip, then clips of at most 120 characters (see Speaking). While the agent speaks, the line playing now is in full colour and every other line is dimmed; when a clip ends the next line lights (a 300 ms CSS colour transition) and the words pane scrolls smoothly to put it in the middle. The highlight changes once per clip, never per frame, so it costs nothing while the voice plays. When nothing is playing every line is in full colour. The pane fades out at its top and bottom edges. There is no word-by-word marker: Browser voices give word timings only through boundary events, and many give none, so guessed timings are not worth having.
- **Barge-in toggle** (Mark 2026-10-13: on the phone's speaker the window kept hearing the agent and cutting it off): a headphones icon button just left of Settings, and the same switch in the settings popover, saved as `__stts__barge` (on by default). On (Headphones icon, "Barge-in on (headset)"): the mic runs while the agent speaks, so speaking over it cuts it off. Off (HeadphoneOff icon, "Barge-in off (mic muted while the agent speaks)"): the mic track is muted and the recogniser stopped for the whole speech, and the listen opens when it ends; typing still cuts the speech off. Each page keeps its own setting, so the phone can be off while the PC with a headset stays on.
- **Buttons:** every button shows its label as a tooltip. Skip (or Esc) stops the speech at once and carries on as if it had finished: a reply with a listen opens the listen, a plain reply returns. Stop still ends the reply and the listen. Repeat plays the last reply again from the window alone (Mark 2026-10-10: to save agent tokens): the browser voice says it again, the daemon is never told, so no turn reaches the agent and an open listen stays open (its no-speech timer restarts when the repeat ends). It is greyed out while speech plays and before any reply. Stop or Skip during a repeat only silences it; words spoken over it go out as a plain turn with no barge line; a new reply from the agent cuts it short. A read-aloud repeats only its last part.
- **Turn cues** (settings, `__stts__cues`): the chimes by default (Mark 2026-10-30: "make them by sound instead by default"), a chime when a listen opens and a tick when his turn is captured; or spoken words, opt-in, "Speak." when a listen opens after the agent's turn and "Stop." when his turn is sent, in the browser voice; or off. Never on an automatic mic restart. A cue word goes into the spoken log and restarts the 3 s echo tail, so the mic never takes it for his words. Cue volume is the old earcon volume, default 0.25 (half the old 0.5; Mark 2026-10-30: quieter cues), for every cue sound (chime, tick, two-tone, cue word); speech volume is separate and unchanged.
- **Themes:** System (follows the OS light or dark setting, the default), Light or Dark, chosen in settings and saved as `__stts__theme`; applied before first paint. Colours and fonts come from the one token file `web/index.css`, mapped to the Mark personal brand (skill design-ui-and-visuals brand.md: the mkb-site oklch palette at tint hue 158, Instrument Sans body, Bricolage Grotesque headings; rule 62). The turn hues of the orb, the turn chip and the wave are `--state-*` tokens there, read once by `web/App.tsx` after the stylesheet loads (`web/main.tsx` imports it first); `test/unit/brand-tokens.test.ts` refuses a raw colour or palette class in `web/*.tsx` (vendored `web/components/ui` exempt). `<meta name="darkreader-lock">` keeps the Dark Reader extension off the window.
- **Controls:** Mute/Unmute, Stop (stops speech and reports `stopped{part}`), Settings, End conversation (sends `ended`; the daemon answers `close` and the window shuts at once, spec 003). Stop and End only act on a real click (`isTrusted`). No spoken word operates the window.
- **Keys:** Ctrl+M or Ctrl+R mute and unmute; Alt+Up and Alt+Down browse history (50 each of his prompts and the agent's replies).
- **Message box:** always shown (Mark 2026-10-10; the old Keyboard / AudioLines toggle is gone). A click anywhere in the window that is not on a control (a button, a field, a menu) puts the cursor in it, so he never has to aim for the box; a drag that selected words to copy keeps its selection. A shadcn Textarea sits at the bottom (placeholder "Message (Enter sends)"; under 360 px wide the Send button drops under it at full width): Enter or the Send button sends, Shift+Enter is a new line, Alt+Up and Alt+Down fill it from his prompt history. Typing while the agent speaks cuts the speech off (barge-in, spec 005).
- **Settings popover:** voice, rate, auto-send, hold, barge-in, mic picker, earcons and their volume, raise-on-request (off by default). Saved in `localStorage` under the old `__stts__*` keys so earlier settings carry over. **Mic picker** (Mark 2026-10-10: the Iriun Webcam mic plugged in after the window opened never showed): the list is read on load, again once the mic stream opens (Chrome gives device names only after a mic grant), and again on every `devicechange`, so a mic plugged in or out while the window is open appears or goes at once. Picking a mic while the mic is open switches to it at once: the open stream is dropped and the recogniser restarts on the new device (log `page mic switched to a new device`), with no reload and no new listen. If the chosen mic is unplugged, the setting falls back to `default` and the open mic follows it; a stream whose device went away is reopened (log `page mic device gone`). An empty list (no grant yet) never resets the choice. Races (e2e 2026-10-30): the mic is read from a ref at open time, a stream opened for a choice that changed while getUserMedia was pending is dropped and the new choice opened, and a newer mic start supersedes an older one still awaiting, so only one recogniser runs.
- **Listening:** The daemon's speech engine (spec 014), fed from one echo-cancelled, noise-suppressed mic stream that is opened once and reused by every start (reopened only when its track dies or the chosen mic changes). Opening a new stream before every start lost his first words, and Chrome's own capture (start() with no track) has no echo cancellation, so the agent's reply came back as his turn (Mark 2026-10-07). A turn's start time is its first speechstart or first words, reset after every turn. The listen-open chime plays first, so it is never recorded.
- **Speaking:** text is cut into clips (`toClips`, `src/sentences.ts`): the first clip is the first sentence alone when it is 40 characters or fewer, else its first clause (a comma, semicolon, colon or dash after at least 8 characters), else the whole sentence, never its words cut at a space (Mark 2026-10-12); the rest are sentences (spec 004) packed into clips of at most 120 characters, a longer sentence cut only at a clause mark. Each clip is spoken with the browser's `speechSynthesis`, one utterance at a time, in the voice chosen in settings or else the best English voice the browser has (a Natural voice, then a Google voice, then any English one); the rate is the slider or the call's `rate`. Chrome stops a long utterance part-way, which short clips avoid. A clip whose utterance errors is logged `voice error REASON` and counts as ended, so the speech never stalls. A new tts that arrives while an earlier one still plays cuts the earlier one off at once and plays only its own clips, with no listen and no `complete` from the cut speech (Mark 2026-10-15: appended, the old audio played on while the highlight followed the new text). The browser voice plays outside the page's audio, so echo cancellation cannot remove it: with barge-in on, use a headset (the setting says so); with it off, the mic is muted while the agent speaks.
- **Earcons:** Kenney Interface Sounds (CC0) in `public/earcon/`: `listen-open`, `turn-captured`, `background-result`, one per state change.
- The 1600x600 strip and a 390 wide phone layout come from Tailwind container queries.
- **On the phone, away from the PC** (Mark 2026-10-13): `tailscale serve --bg --https=443 http://127.0.0.1:15986` puts the page at `https://<pc>.<tailnet>.ts.net/`, tailnet only. The page opens its `/ws` socket with `wss` when it was loaded over https, so the phone's own mic and speaker carry the conversation. The page that attached last gets the turns. An agent never closes or kills the stts window on its own (Mark 2026-10-13): he may be using it from his phone; if both pages could feed turns, the agent asks him which one he is on. `tailscale serve --https=443 off` ends it.

The status icon turns red when the mic has failed (spec 005).

## Journeys

One user type: the person talking to the agent. Each e2e test starts a fresh Playwright browser context, so every row is also a new-device, cleared-browser visit.

| User | Entry point | Device or visit | Failure | Path back | e2e test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| speaker | agent calls `stt` | new device, fresh profile | none | n/a, turn returned | `a listen produces a turn` |
| speaker | types in the box | new device | voice not usable | typed text is the turn | `a typed message is returned by stt as a turn` |
| speaker | agent speaks, he talks over it | return visit, window already open | barge misjudged as echo | his words still cut it off | `speaking over the agent cuts it off, a filler included; its own echo does not` |
| speaker | pauses mid-sentence | return visit | second half lost | both parts join into one turn | `a pause mid-sentence loses nothing: both parts arrive, short or long pause` |
| speaker | mic unplugged | return visit | open mic gone | falls back to the default mic | `a mic plugged in later is listed, picking it switches the open mic, unplugging it falls back` |
| speaker | presses End | return visit | agent keeps calling | next call reopens the window | `End reaches the open listen once; the next call carries on` |
| speaker | narrow window | new device, small screen | layout breaks | Send moves under the box | `at a narrow width the Send button sits under the box, and the box keeps the row` |

## What it reads and writes

Reads the mic and the daemon's WebSocket messages. Writes `localStorage` settings and history, and sends page messages (spec 001) back to the daemon.

## How to run, check and hand over

`npm run dev` serves the page with Vite. `test/screenshots/shoot.ts <url> [size prefix]` produces the screenshots in `test/screenshots/` at 1600x600 and 390 wide (a size prefix such as `light` shoots one set, so a run fits in 60 s); check them for layout and for no console errors. It also fails when, two clips into a reply, the lit third line is not across the middle of the words pane (`*-speaking-later.png`). `test/unit/page-log.test.ts` checks the page's log lines.

The window icon is the lucide `audio-waveform` glyph (ISC licence) in blue `#3b82f6` on a transparent background, with no tile (Mark 2026-10-06: the old orb on a dark square looked dated). Source is `public/favicon.svg`; `favicon.ico` (16, 24, 32, 48, 64 px), `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png` and `apple-touch-icon.png` were generated from it with the `favicons` npm package 7.3.1 (not a dependency), and `icon-256.png` with the `sharp` it brings. `index.html` and `manifest.webmanifest` link them with a `?v=N` that is bumped with every icon change (Chrome keeps a window's icon by URL, so the taskbar kept the old one after a reload), and the Chrome `--app` window takes its taskbar icon from them; it shows on the next window open. To change the icon, edit the SVG and rerun both.
